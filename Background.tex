\section{Concluding Remarks}

This chapter has reviewed the essential theoretical foundations for evaluating MRI safety in patients with retained epicardial leads. Epicardial leads, integral to CIEDs, differ from transvenous leads in placement, design (unipolar vs.\ bipolar, coaxial vs.\ coradial), fixation (suturing vs.\ screwing), and material composition (paramagnetic electrodes and conductors; diamagnetic insulation). Their direct attachment to the epicardium and frequent abandonment (often as 5--30\,cm fragments, 0.5--1.5\,mm diameter) heighten concern for mechanical interactions with MRI fields, despite low-to-moderate magnetic susceptibilities that suggest minimal risk.

MRI fundamentals, static field ($B_0$), RF excitation, gradient encoding, and relaxation ($T_1$, $T_2$), were explained aiming to provide enough information to assess the interaction with implants. The emphasis was on the static field, capable of inducing translational forces and torques on conductive implants, and the physics that rule the interaction seeking to characterize such forces.

Current guidelines (HRS 2017, ESC 2021, CMS) remain conservative, considering systems with abandoned or epicardial leads non--MRI-conditional due to insufficient data, despite evidence that post-2000 leads may be safe under monitoring \cite{vuorinen2021,schaller2021}. This gap motivates the present experimental study. By quantifying static-field forces and torques in controlled settings, using ASTM-based pendulum and torque-transfer methods (resolving forces and torque to $\sim$10$^{-6}$\,N), the work aims to challenge restrictive policies and enable evidence-based updates to clinical MRI protocols for this growing patient population.
