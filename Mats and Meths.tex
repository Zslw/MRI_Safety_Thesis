This chapter details the experimental setups and measurement procedures developed to quantify \gls{mri}-induced translational forces and torque acting on post-surgical epicardial pacing lead when exposed to its static magnetic field. Three non-magnetic test fixtures were constructed, specifically designed to measure these forces with the precision required for clinical applications, in accordance to ASTM guidelines \cite{astm2213,astmF2052,astmF2182,ASTM_F2503_2023}. First, a spatial field mapping platform was fabricated to characterize $B_0$ and $\nabla B_0$. Alongside but independently, a pendulum-based deflection apparatus was made to assess translational force through angular displacement. Finally, a strain sensor with a low-friction pole and lever arm measurement system were designed, cut, and assembled for rotational force (torque) assessments. 

All three fixtures were developed to be tested in a laboratory framework first. As such, this chapter is divided into the three experiments listed above. Each, constituted of three parts, a technical description of the fixtures developed, the in-lab testing, and an initial clinical methodology. We also outline the design rationale, key modifications, and resulting improvements in sensitivity and precision to support reproducibility.

In the laboratory, the complete suite of instruments and associated control/acquisition software was constructed and tested in a controlled low-field environment (in range of 30 - 300 G). Although this field strength is two to three orders of magnitude below the 3 T clinical scanner, it was deliberately chosen to enable precise evaluation of the fixtures’ accuracy, sensitivity, resolution, and repeatability without requiring scanner time. The laboratory setup also allowed direct observation of the fundamental physical phenomena described in Sections \ref{sec:translationalForceBG} and \ref{sec:torqueBG} on representative test samples.

Importantly, both translational force and torque scale predictably with the magnetic field strength, $F \propto B_0 \, \frac{dB_0}{dz}$ and $\tau \propto B_0^2$. Consequently, the dimensionless relationships in the behavior remain identical between the laboratory and clinical regimes. We reproduced the same normalized force and torque signatures that will be encountered in the \gls{mri} suite by scaling the effective susceptibility $  \chi_m  $ and density $  \rho  $ of the test objects (see Appendix \ref{app:scaling}), for use under small magnetic field. The laboratory results are therefore directly valuable. They confirmed that the instruments function as intended and that the physics is captured correctly before any clinical measurements were attempted.

As outlined in Section \ref{sec:Scope}, the several constraints of clinical \gls{mri} access necessitated thorough laboratory validation of the instruments and protocols beforehand. Through institutional collaboration, CHI CUMC Bergan Mercy Hospital, accommodated a dedicated measurement session on their Siemens 3T Magnetom Trio\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} \gls{mri} scanner. Figure \ref{fig:scanner} depicts the scanner in which measurements were conducted under supervision to ensure safety protocol. Accordingly, the clinical session pursued the two complementary goals previously defined, namely to verify device performance and identify workflow issues. Moreover, a clinical session provided the first dataset on the \gls{mri}-induced translational forces and torques acting on post-surgical epicardial pacing leads, thereby closing the loop between bench validation and in-vivo applicability.

\section{Experimental Setup Summary}

This chapter outlined the development and validation of three custom non-magnetic test fixtures designed to quantify \gls{mri}-induced translational forces and torques on post-surgical epicardial pacing leads, in accordance with ASTM standards\cite{astmF2052,astm2213,astmF2182,ASTM_F2503_2023}. Each fixture was tested in a low-field laboratory environment ($\sim$30 G) using scaled ferromagnetic samples, before initial clinical trials on a Siemens Magnetom Trio\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} 3T \gls{mri} scanner at CHI CUMC Bergan Mercy Hospital. The setups prioritized precision, sensitivity, and reproducibility.

The first fixture focused on characterizing the static magnetic field strength and gradient. In the lab fields were generated via a custom air-core solenoid and probe was positioned using a translational stage with servomotors for sampling. In the clinic, the platform and probe were mounted on the patient couch, aligned to isocenter and translated axially his providing volumetric field maps essential for force/torque predictions.

The second fixture employed a pendulum-based deflection apparatus (ASTM F2052-15 \cite{astmF2052}) to assess forces through angular displacement balanced against gravity. In the lab, we used the previously mention solenoid and deflection angles were measured and converted to forces. Additionally, we established the lower limit of resolved forces down to millinewtons, which were constrained by the shape and accuracy of the ruler. In the clinic, the fixture was positioned on the patient table and incrementally advanced into the bore entrance. Deflections on cylinder-shaped samples (316L stainless steel and titanium) and leads were recorded manually.

Finally, the third fixture used a pole and low-friction bearing with transfer arms, algonside a strain-sensor to quantify torque. In the lab, sensors were calibrated by applying known weights, establishing linear force-voltage relationships. Then, the smallest measurable torque was analyzed based on rod stiffness and signal resolution. The setup included solenoid/neodymium fields, DAQ system, and LabVIEW for data acquisition. In the clinic, fixture was aligned on the couch, the test objects were oriented to maximize response and torque on non-magnetic samples was documented, with plans for refinement.

With these setups the transition from laboratory to clinical is completed. Workflow layout, instrument operation validation, and fundamental physics is laid out. Furthermore, a preliminary clinical data on epicardial leads was obtain. Next, Chapter \ref{ch:results} shows all findings related to each experiment in laboratory and clinical settings, and Chapter \ref{ch:disCon} discusses validity of our findings and post-clinical notes on experiment improvement.
