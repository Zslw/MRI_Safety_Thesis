This chapter details the experimental setups and measurement procedures developed to quantify \gls{mri}-induced translational forces and torque acting on post-surgical epicardial pacing lead when exposed to its static magnetic field. 

Three non-magnetic test fixtures were constructed, specifically designed to measure these forces with the precision required for clinical applications, in accordance to ASTM guidelines \cite{astm2213,astmF2052,astmF2182,ASTM_F2503_2023}. First, a spatial field mapping platform to characterize $B_0$ and $\nabla B_0$. Alongside but independently, a pendulum-based deflection apparatus to assess translational force through angular displacement. Finally, a strain sensor with a low-friction pole and lever arm measurement system for rotational force (torque) assessments. 

All three fixtures were developed to be tested in a laboratory framework first. As such, this chapter is divided into the three experiments we described. Each, constituted of three parts, a technical description of the fixtures developed, the in-lab testing, and a first clinical methodology. As we explain our setup, rationale, and proven modifications to the instruments, their sensitivity, and precision, in order to provide a reproducible map of action to test our instruments. 

In the laboratory, the complete suite of instruments and associated control/acquisition software was constructed and tested in a controlled low-field environment ($\approx$ 30 G). Although this field strength is two orders of magnitude below the 3 T clinical scanner, it was deliberately chosen to enable precise evaluation of the fixtures’ accuracy, sensitivity, resolution, and repeatability without requiring scanner time. The laboratory setup also allowed direct observation of the fundamental physical phenomena described in Sections \ref{sec:translationalForceBG} and \ref{sec:torqueBG} on representative ferromagnetic test samples.

Importantly, both translational force and torque scale predictably with the magnetic field strength, $F \propto B_0 \, \frac{dB_0}{dz}$ and $\tau \propto B_0^2$. Consequently, the dimensionless relationships in the behavior remain identical between the laboratory (30 G) and clinical (3 T) regimes. We reproduce the same normalized force and torque signatures that will be encountered in the \gls{mri} suite by scaling the effective susceptibility $  \chi_m  $ and density $  \rho  $ of the test objects (see Appendix \ref{app:scaling}), that is using ferromagnetic objects under small magnetic field. The laboratory results are therefore directly valuable, they confirm that the instruments function as intended and that the physics is captured correctly before any clinical measurements are attempted.

As outlined in section \ref{sec:Scope} , the severe constraints of clinical \gls{mri} access necessitated thorough laboratory validation of the instruments and protocols beforehand. Accordingly, the clinical session pursued the two complementary goals previously defined, verify device performance and identify workflow issues. Moreover, a clinical session provided the first dataset on the \gls{mri}-induced translational forces and torques acting on post-surgical epicardial pacing leads, thereby closing the loop between bench validation and in-vivo applicability.
