All six coordinates exhibited the expected monotonic increase in field strength as the probe approached the bore entrance, with $B$ decreasing by approximately 2.5--2.9 T over the full 1252 mm couch travel. At the bore entrance, peak field strengths ranged from 2.51 T at G6 to 2.95 T at A6, consistent with the scanner's 3 T isocenter field attenuating into the fringe region sampled by the fixture. 

The spatial gradient $|\nabla B|$ ranged from 5.96 T/m at G6 to 10.39 T/m at A6, and the force-relevant product $|B \nabla B|$ ranged from 12.68 T$^2$/m at G6 to 22.95 T$^2$/m at A6.  The near-axis coordinates (00, G3, G6) agreed within approximately 2\% in peak $B$, confirming lateral homogeneity in that region and consistent with the homogeneity observed in the solenoid mapping of Section~\ref{sec:resMapSol}.

G6, located 100 mm below isocenter along the vertical axis toward the patient table, returned the lowest $|B \nabla B|$ product among all coordinates (12.68 T$^2$/m). While not the position of maximum field gradient, it represents the most physically relevant location for the translational force measurements. It lies closest to where an implanted epicardial lead would sit relative to the scanner bore during a clinical scan. The G6 coordinate therefore yielded the field conditions  ($|B_0| = 2.51$ T and $|\nabla B| = 5.96$ T/m) used as the test location input for the ASTM F2052-15\cite{astmF2052} extrapolation in Section~\ref{sec:resClinicTrans}.
