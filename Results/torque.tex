\subsection{Torque from Lab scale magnet}

As described in section \ref{sec:InLab_Torque}, torque measurements were performed under two different experimental configurations. When a small uniform magnetic field was applied using Helmholtz coils, the torque produced by the short (2 cm) magnetic test cylinder remained at or below the detection limit of the measurement system. Only the longer rod yielded reliable results. Figure \ref{fig:torque_longerRod} shows the maximum torque (N m) against the magnetic flux experimental data (in blue) exhibiting a parabolic dependence on $  B  $. The data was fit to a full parabola and a zero intercept parabola with the objective to assess the circuit's tare contribution.

\begin{figure}[htb!]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Assets/fig_torque_LR.png}
	\caption[Torque vs. magnetic field strength for the longer cylinder under Helmholtz coils]{Maximum measured torque in mNm as a function of applied magnetic flux density in mB for the longer cylinder under a Helmholtz coil configuration. On the left, a zero intercept fit. Right, a full quadratic fit.}
	\label{fig:torque_longerRod}
\end{figure}

First with zero intercept, $k    = +379.36  \pm  8.6277 (2.3\%)$ while the full quadratic fit yielded, $k = 386.55 \pm 49.7 (12.9\%)$, $b = -0.054631$ and $V_0 = 8.40 \times 10 ^{-5}$. The dominant quadratic term ($  k B^2  $) is in agreement with the theoretical expectation for the torque on a magnetic dipole in a uniform field, where $  \tau \propto B^2  $. The linear coefficient $  b  $ is very small and statistically consistent with zero within uncertainty, while the offset $  V_0  $ is negligible, confirming the absence of significant systematic bias for this measurement. All fitting parameters are in SI units no scaling. 

Because of the difficulty quantifying torque for the short cylinder (2 cm) under the Helmholtz coil, a second set of measurements was performed using significantly stronger magnetic fields generated by four neodymium magnets. Figure \ref{fig:torque_shorterRod} shows a parabolic relationship. Notice that the values on torque are smaller for higher magnetic fields.

\begin{figure}[htb!]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{Assets/fig_torque_SR_neo.png}
	\caption[Torque dependence on magnetic field strength for 2 cm cylinder under neodymium magnet fields]{Maximum torque in mNm as a function of applied magnetic flux density in mT for the shorter (2cm) magnetic cylinder under stronger uniform fields (Neodymium magnets). Blue points is the experimental measurements while orange curve represents a quadratic fit. On the left, a zero intercept fit. Right, a full quadratic fit.}
	\label{fig:torque_shorterRod}
\end{figure}

This time the fits yielded, a zero intercept $k = +7.008  \pm  0.2403 (3.4\%)$ and full parabola parameters $k = +17.547 \pm 3.274 (18.7\%)$, $b = -0.44248$ and $V_0 = +4.36 \times 10 ^{-3}$. When a non-zero offset $V_0$ is present the zero intercept model absorbs part of the offset into $k$, biasing it downward. When $V_0$ is genuine noise both models give equivalent information.

\subsection{Torque from 3T Siemens Magnetom\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} \gls{mri} scanner}

Once in a clinical environment, both the circuit and the \gls{mri}-safe torque measuring fixture were functioning properly and circuit calibration was appropriate for clinical measurements. Based on the magnetic field map acquired in the same session (Section~\ref{sec:FieldGeneration}), the maximum field experienced by the samples during the closest positioning was approximately $B \approx 0.5$ T at $\sim$0.4 m from the bore face.

Two types of cylindrical rod samples were tested. One type exhibited a clear response when brought near a strong permanent magnet prior to the session, suggesting a ferromagnetic contribution. The other showed no such response, suspected to be  a weakly paramagnetic or diamagnetic material. However, neither type produced any perceptible or sensor-measurable torque deflection at any position along the couch travel path.

No torque-induced angular shift was detected on the sensor readout for either sample type across all positioning attempts. The signal remained within the noise baseline throughout, with no systematic deflection observed as field strength increased.

The absence of a measurable response is not readily explained by the sensor detection limit alone. The smallest torque resolved by the sensor in calibration was $\tau_{\rm min} = {1.24e-5}{Nm}$ (Section~\ref{sec:calResult}). For a ferromagnetic cylinder of approximately 20 cm length and 4 mm diameter positioned at 0.5 T, the predicted torque from the scaling analysis exceeds $\tau_{\rm min}$ by several orders of magnitude. The null result therefore requires further investigation, and possible explanations are addressed in Chapter~\ref{ch:disCon}.
