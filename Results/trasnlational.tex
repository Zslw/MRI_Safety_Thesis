\subsection{Translational forces observed in a 3T Siemens Magnetom\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} \gls{mri} scanner}
\label{sec:resClinicTrans}

% ── CLINICAL TRANSLATIONAL SECTION TEXT ──────────────────────────

In the clinic, four test objects were tested following the ASTM F2052-15\cite{astmF2052} pendulum protocol at the bore entrance of the 3T Siemens Magnetom\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} scanner. A 101 cm pendulum was used, with displacement uncertainty $\delta x = 0.05$ cm. The ASTM F2052-15 acceptance criterion is a force ratio $\mathrm{FR} = \tan(\alpha) < 1.0$ (equivalently, $\alpha < 45^\circ$), which corresponds to the magnetically induced displacement force being less than the gravitational weight of the device \cite{astmF2052}.

Figure~\ref{fig:translational_clinic_res} shows the epicardial lead mounted on the pendulum fixture at the bore entrance. A visible displacement of up to 9 cm was recorded for the Medtronic STREAMLINE 6942 bipolar epicardial lead, making it the most strongly deflected device among those tested.

Figure~\ref{fig:F2052_FR_vs_position} shows the force ratio $\mathrm{FR} = \tan(\alpha)$ as a function of z-axis position along the bore axis for all four devices. The force ratio increases monotonically as the device approaches the bore entrance (smaller z), consistent with the increasing $B|\nabla B|$ product expected from the magnetic field map. Critically, all devices remained well below the $\mathrm{FR} = 1.0$ threshold across the entire measured range. The Medtronic STREAMLINE 6942 bipolar epicardial lead and the 316L stainless steel reference sample reached the highest force ratios, with maximum mean values of $\tan(\alpha) = 0.0931$ ($\alpha = 5.32^\circ$) and $\tan(\alpha) = 0.0905$ ($\alpha = 5.17^\circ$), respectively. The titanium coil and titanium straight rod showed substantially lower deflections, with maximum mean force ratios of $\tan(\alpha) = 0.0092$ ($\alpha = 0.53^\circ$) and $\tan(\alpha) = 0.0040$ ($\alpha = 0.23^\circ$), the Ti straight wire showed minimal deflection making its tendency effectively flat.

Figure~\ref{fig:comparison_gradient} presents the allowable maximum spatial gradient derived from the ASTM F2052-15 Appendix X3 extrapolation (Eq.~X3.9)\cite{astmF2052}, which projects measured deflection angles at the test location onto the clinical scanner conditions of 1.5 T and 3 T. Angles below 2$^\circ$ are floored to 2$^\circ$ for conservative extrapolation, this floor was applied to the Ti Coil and Ti Rod results. The extrapolated allowable spatial gradients substantially exceed the typical scanner limits reported, namely $19$ T/m for 1.5 T systems and $17$ T/m for 3.0 T systems for all four devices. Of most interest, the bipolar lead yields an allowable gradient of 107 T/m at 1.5 T and 54 T/m at 3.0 T, both far beyond the scanner's physical maximum gradient, indicating that the translational force alone does not pose a safety concern under standard clinical conditions.

It should be noted, that per F2052-15 \cite{astmF2052}, a passing result for translational force is not sufficient alone to establish MR safety. RF-induced heating (ASTM F2182 \cite{astmF2182}) and torque (ASTM F2213 \cite{astm2213}) must also be assessed. The Ti Rod and 316L stainless steel samples were tested in a single trial only, falling short of the three-repetition minimum required by the standard. These results should therefore be treated as indicative rather than fully compliant.
