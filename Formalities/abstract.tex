%  Revised Abstract — Daniel Juarez Luna, M.S. Thesis
%  Magnetically Induced Forces on Epicardial Leads
%  Creighton University, March 2026
% ============================================================
Retained epicardial pacing leads following cardiac surgery are labeled MRI-unsafe due to risks of static-field translational forces and torques, despite frequent diagnostic need for MRI in this patient population. This thesis developed and clinically tested three custom non-magnetic fixtures to quantify these interactions at micro-Newton scales: (1) a PVC mapping platform for $B$ and $\nabla B$ characterization, (2) an ASTM~F2052-15 pendulum for displacement force, and (3) a strain-gauge cantilever sensor inspired by ASTM~F2213-17 for torque.

Each fixture was validated in a controlled laboratory environment using a custom air-core solenoid and Helmholtz/neodymium fields ($\leq$180\,G), then deployed on a 3\,T Siemens Magnetom\texttrademark{} scanner at CHI Health CUMC--Bergan Mercy, Omaha, NE. Laboratory field mapping confirmed the expected field spatial decay, establishing the product needed for force predictions. Trial pendulum measurements of ferromagnetic sample yielded $\chi_m/\rho = (2.01 \pm 0.05)\times10^{-3}$\,m$^3$/kg ($\tan\alpha = 0.002$--$0.012$), validating the linear force model before clinical deployment. Torque sensor calibration produced consistent linear voltage-to-force slopes across all orientations ($\tau_{\min} = 1.24\times10^{-5}\,N \cdot m$), and laboratory measurements confirmed $\tau \propto B^{2}$ dependence ($k = 379.4 \pm 8.6$\,N/m, 2.3\%).

Clinical field mapping yielded $|B\nabla B|$ between $12.68$ and $22.95$\,T$^2$/m at the bore entrance. Translational-force ratios ($\mathrm{FR} = \tan\alpha$) remained below the ASTM~F2052-15 threshold of 1.0 for all devices. The Medtronic STREAMLINE\textsuperscript{\texttrademark}~6492 unipolar epicardial lead reached the highest deflection ($\alpha = 5.32^{\circ}$, $\mathrm{FR} = 0.093$), ten times larger than titanium references ($\mathrm{FR} = 0.004$--$0.009$) yet well within the safety criterion. ASTM~F2052-15 extrapolation yielded allowable gradients of $107$\,T/m at 1.5\,T and $54$\,T/m at 3.0\,T for the unipolar lead, both far exceeding the scanner's physical maximum ($\leq 19$\,T/m). Clinical torque runs at $B \approx 1.5$\,T produced no measurable deflection, a null result requiring further investigation.

The results indicate that micro-Newton-scale magnetic-field mechanical effects on the tested epicardial lead can be measured under 3,T conditions, though the findings are limited to a single configuration and do not support general safety conclusions. Reproducible micro-Newton-sensitive measurements are possible for MRI safety assessment, establishing a methodology that can be extended to evaluate a broader range of lead designs and conditions.
