\subsubsection{Smallest measurable translational force}
\label{sec:Smallestmeasurable}

\begin{wrapfigure}[11]{R}{5.9cm}
	%\vspace{-30pt}
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.10\textwidth, keepaspectratio]{Assets/leastmeasure.png}
	\caption[Pendulum geometry at equilibrium.]{Right-angled triangle formed by the pendulum geometry at equilibrium. The length of the string $L$ and the pendulum's position $z$ define the deflection angle $\alpha$ that a test object experiences.}
	\label{fig:triangleDiagram}
	%\vspace{10pt}
\end{wrapfigure}

The smallest measurable translational force can be obtained by considering the geometry of the device. The pendulum from Figure \ref{fig:realpendulum_full} forms a right triangle where the vertical line is the pole \textit{``L"}, the horizontal gives the position on the pendulum \textit{``z"} and the hypotenuse is the pendulum's string. Both of these measurements have a $1 mm$ precision, yielding a $L=1 \text{m}$ and $z=1 \text{mm}$ triangle (Figure \ref{fig:triangleDiagram}). Then, the smallest deflection angle capable of measurement is given by $\alpha=\tan(z/L) \sim z/L$ for small angle which is about of a $10^{-3}$ rad ($\sim$ 0.057 deg). 

Since the angular deflection corresponds to the ratio of magnetic to gravitational forces the smallest measurable magnetic force is 

\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:minForce}
	F_{m,min} = \tan(\alpha_{min})= mg (\frac{z}{L}).
\end{equation}

For a 1 g test mass, this corresponds to a detection threshold of $F_{m,min} \sim 10^{-6}N$ ($1\mu N$). Thus, the pendulum fixture can resolve magnetic force as small as one-thousandth of the object’s weight. This resolution defines the lower limit of measurable magnetic susceptibility using the pendulum method. For nonmagnetic or weakly paramagnetic materials, such sensitivity ensures that even subtle translational forces can be quantified, making the setup suitable for assessing device safety under \gls{mri} static field gradients. Sample calculations on this can be found on appendix \ref{app:smallestForce}.
